A survey of European and American literature and culture from the Enlightenment through the present, _Modern Masterpieces of Western Culture_ encourages critical reflection on each of its titular keywords: Modern, Masterpiece, Western, and Culture.

The course tells an overarching story about how the "West" has used literature to reflect on and create various images of itself as "modern" and, often, distinct from the rest of the world. In practical terms, this means acquainting students by turns with each of the major aesthetic movements since the 18<sup>th</sup> century, such as Romanticism, Realism, and Modernism. By reading classic texts from each period against the backdrop of Europe's evolving colonial and capitalist projects, the course allows students to appreciate the big picture of how texts relate to their historical context. At the same time, it reveals that literature itself is an ever-changing entity with shifting ambitions. With this high-level overview, students can begin to see the complex, fraught histories woven into the cultural works that they consume today.
