This course introduces students to the pleasures and challenges of world literature. Taking premodern texts from a range of traditions, the course raises questions about what literature is and what it can tell us about the cultures that produce it. Students learn overarching categories — like folk tales, courtly literature, liturgical texts — that can be applied across cultures and time frames to begin to draw connections and distinctions. They also learn some basic terminology of translation theory that is useful when discussing “foreign” literatures. They learn techniques for finding their way in unfamiliar genres and aesthetic contexts, rather than simply giving up or awaiting expert advice. Finally, they reflect on the conceptual and political challenges of lumping such diverse texts and practices into one overarching (and specifically modern and European) notion of literature.
