title: "Life in a Dead Language: Modern Sanskrit as an Ultraminor Literature"
venue: Journal of World Literature
year: "2017"
link: https://doi.org/10.1163/24056480-00204004
abstract: Literature written in Sanskrit after the onset of British colonialism
  is sorely neglected. Modern Sanskrit, as it is often called, suffers from the
  bad image of being written in a dead language. Many of its writers would
  disagree with that image, but they would know that they are disagreeing. That
  defensiveness has come to shape their writing, a fact which I argue arises in
  response to the status of their work as an ultraminor literature, a status
  which was born with the formation of the “world literature” field and its
  elevation/absorption of classical Sanskrit at the expense of the latter’s
  perceived potential for contemporaneity.
